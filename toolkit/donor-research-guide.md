# Donor Research Guide — Finding Local Supporters for Classroom Supplies

This guide helps you identify local businesses, community organizations, and individuals who may be willing to support a classroom supply need — without buying lists, scraping contact info, or sending bulk cold messages.

## Step 1: Map your local supporter landscape

Create a simple list of organizations within 5-10 miles of your school. Do not gather personal contact information for individuals you don't already know. Focus on publicly listed business/organization contact info.

| Category | Examples | How to find them |
|---|---|---|
| Local businesses near school | Hardware store, grocery, pharmacy, restaurant | Google Maps "near [school address]" |
| Businesses run by school families | Any business where a parent/grandparent works or owns | School directory, PTO member knowledge |
| Community service clubs | Rotary, Kiwanis, Lions Club, Elks Lodge | "[City] Rotary Club" search |
| Religious/civic organizations | Churches, temples, community centers near the school | Google Maps, community bulletin boards |
| Local foundations | Community foundation, education foundation | "[County/City] community foundation" |
| Corporate giving programs | Big-box stores with community grant programs (Walmart, Target, etc.) | Company website "community giving" pages |

## Step 2: Prioritize by likelihood and relationship

Not every organization needs to be contacted. Prioritize:

1. **Existing relationships first.** Does a parent work there? Has the business donated before? Has a teacher or staff member connected with them?
2. **Proximity.** Businesses within walking distance or the same ZIP code are more likely to say yes.
3. **Alignment.** A children's bookstore is more likely to donate books than a car dealership.

## Step 3: Find the right contact point

For each prioritized organization, find **one** publicly listed contact method:

- **Local businesses:** "Contact Us" page on their website, or the general email/phone listed on Google Maps.
- **Service clubs:** Their website usually lists a president or community service chair with a public email.
- **Foundations:** Grant application page or general inquiry email.
- **Corporate programs:** The specific "community giving" or "donation request" page — do not email the CEO.

Never scrape, never buy lists, never use personal emails found outside public business listings.

## Step 4: Prepare your one-paragraph ask

Before reaching out to anyone, have ready:
- Your specific supply need (items, quantities, approximate cost)
- Your authorized wishlist or drop-off path
- A one-paragraph email/message template (see the email templates in the main toolkit)
- A simple tracker to record who you contacted and when

## Step 5: Respect "no" immediately

If someone says no, thank them and do not follow up again for at least 6 months. Never argue, guilt-trip, or "just check if they changed their mind."

## Compliance checklist before any outreach

- [ ] I am authorized by my school/group to make this request.
- [ ] The organization's contact info is publicly listed for business inquiries.
- [ ] I am sending one message to one public contact point, not bulk messaging.
- [ ] I have a specific supply need with quantities, not a vague "anything helps."
- [ ] I can explain exactly how supplies/funds will be used and confirmed.
- [ ] I am not claiming nonprofit status my group does not have.
