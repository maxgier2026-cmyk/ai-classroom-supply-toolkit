# Classroom Wishlist Setup Guide

A step-by-step guide to creating, sharing, and managing a classroom supply wishlist that is clear, specific, and easy for supporters to act on.

## Platform options (choose one that your school/district allows)

| Platform | Best for | Cost | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon Wishlist | General supplies, books | Free | Widely recognized, easy to share link |
| Walmart Registry | General supplies | Free | Good for communities without easy Amazon access |
| Target Registry | General supplies, classroom decor | Free | In-store pickup option for local donors |
| DonorsChoose | Larger projects, tech, furniture | Free (donors cover small fulfillment fee) | Specifically for teachers; public searchable |
| Google Sheets (public view) | Custom tracking, multiple store links | Free | More work to set up but flexible |

## Step 1: Audit what you actually need

Before creating any wishlist, walk through your classroom and list:
- Items running low (pencils, glue sticks, tissues)
- Items completely out (specific books, art supplies)
- Items you've been borrowing or going without
- Upcoming project needs for the next 1-3 months

Do not add items "just in case" — a focused list of 10-20 items converts better than 50 random items.

## Step 2: Choose quantities and price points

Include a range of price points so there's something for every budget:

| Price range | Examples | Why include |
|---|---|---|
| Under $5 | Pencil pack, glue sticks, dry-erase markers | Quick impulse support |
| $5-$15 | Notebook set, art supplies, book | Most common donation range |
| $15-$30 | Classroom book set, storage bins | Committed supporters |
| $30-$75 | Document camera, headphones, special equipment | Sponsorships and group gifts |

## Step 3: Write a clear wishlist description

Your wishlist title and description should answer three questions immediately:

**Title example:** "Ms. Johnson's 3rd Grade Classroom — Fall 2026 Supplies"

**Description template:**
> Welcome to our classroom wishlist! Every item goes directly to 3rd grade students at [School Name]. I update this list monthly based on what we're actually running low on — no random extras.
>
> **Current top priorities:** [List 3 items with brief why]
> **Please note:** [Any district rules, e.g., no food items, no used items, etc.]
> **Thank you for supporting our classroom!**

## Step 4: Set up tracking

Create a simple tracking sheet (Google Sheets or paper):

| Item | Requested qty | Fulfilled qty | Remaining | Donor (optional) | Thank-you sent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pencils, 24-pack | 5 | 2 | 3 | — | — |
| Notebooks | 30 | 15 | 15 | — | — |

Update this weekly so you always know what's still needed.

## Step 5: Share the wishlist — where and when

**Best channels:**
- PTO/PTA newsletter or Facebook group
- Your classroom newsletter/communication app (Remind, ClassDojo, etc.)
- School website "Support Our Classrooms" page
- Local community Facebook groups (with admin permission)
- Beginning-of-year open house / parent night flyer

**When to share:**
- Beginning of school year (July-September): biggest response window
- After winter break (January): mid-year restock
- Before specific projects: "We're starting our science unit next month and need..."
- Teacher Appreciation Week (May): supporters actively looking to give
- Back-to-school sales season (July-August): supplies are cheapest

**When NOT to share:**
- More than once per month to the same audience
- During school-wide fundraising campaigns (coordinate with PTO)
- During holiday gift drives (your wishlist will get buried)

## Step 6: Thank and update

After items arrive:
1. Send a thank-you within 48 hours (template in main toolkit).
2. Update your tracking sheet.
3. Post a privacy-safe classroom update once per quarter: "Thanks to our supporters, we received XX pencils, XX notebooks, and XX art supply kits this quarter."

Never post photos of students opening supplies or name individual students in connection with donations.
